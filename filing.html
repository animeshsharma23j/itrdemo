    Extends a UX case study on India's ITR filing experience. The four profiles above mirror four sample AIS statements built to test the idea: a clean salaried case, multiple capital-gains rows plus an unconfirmed one, a freelancer with several TDS deductors, and a business case with an unlinked tax challan — the exact "paid but not reflected" gap the original research surfaced. Idea stage only.

    Tax math: new regime, FY 2025-26 slabs, with the ₹75,000 salaried standard deduction, the section 87A rebate and marginal relief up to ₹12,00,000 in slab-rate income, equity STCG at 20%, equity LTCG at 12.5% above a pooled ₹1,25,000/year exemption, presumptive income at 50% of receipts (44ADA) or 6% of digital turnover (44AD) where relevant, and 4% health & education cess. Surcharge applies above ₹50L total income — 10% (₹50L–1Cr), 15% (₹1Cr–2Cr), capped at 25% beyond that under the new regime — with equity capital-gains surcharge separately capped at 15%. Marginal relief is modeled at the ₹12L rebate cliff but not at the surcharge thresholds; old-regime deductions and other edge cases are left out. This demonstrates the mechanic, not a filing-grade calculator.
